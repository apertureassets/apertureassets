Destination

South Korea

14 days · £2,300

I travelled from 9th to 23rd May, which turned out to be a good window for visiting South Korea. It's cheaper than the summer months, and you get the weather sweet spot too, warm enough to enjoy being outside without the intense heat or humidity that rolls in later, and well ahead of the rainy season that tends to hit from late June. That said, the rain caught up with me towards the end of the trip, a full wet day in Busan and more on arrival into Jeonju, so it isn't entirely dry even in this window. The trip took me through Seoul, Gyeongju, Busan and Jeonju, before finishing back in Seoul ahead of the flight home from Incheon.

Travel Notes

Before You Fly

There's currently a visa waiver system in place for UK visitors, with the K-ETA not required for stays under six months until December 2026. It's worth double checking this before you travel though, as these exemptions do get extended or changed. Instead, you'll need to complete an e-Arrival Card, which can only be submitted up to three days before your arrival date. I did mine ahead of flying out, and being able to retrieve the submission at immigration made for a smooth process after an 11.5 hour flight.

I flew with Virgin Atlantic direct from Heathrow to Incheon, departing at 12:30pm and landing at 10:30am the following day. I paid £752 for Economy Delight, which for around an extra £70 gets you slightly more legroom. Worth noting the seats themselves were uncomfortable both ways regardless, so the upgrade is really just for the extra space rather than any meaningful comfort gain. The ticket included 23kg checked bags each way, plus a carry on.

For the KTX bullet trains, book ahead if you can. Availability was already getting limited on some routes around two weeks before I flew out. They're excellent value compared to the UK's train network, speed, efficiency and price all comfortably ahead. I'd recommend travelling with carry on sized luggage, similar to Japan, so you can stow it overhead rather than relying on dedicated luggage space, which for the most part wasn't there when boarding at each stop. Worth flagging if you've done the Shinkansen before, the KTX carriages are noticeably smaller in terms of passenger space by comparison.

Before you land, I'd again recommend an eSIM that activates automatically on arrival. I used Airalo, opting for 10GB over 15 days at £15. WiFi is available almost everywhere in South Korea, on buses, trains and the subway, so you're rarely relying on your own data.

Travel Notes

On The Ground Essentials

For local transport payments, pick up a T-money card from a convenience store as soon as you land in Incheon. Mine cost around KRW3,000 for the card itself, and you top it up as you go at convenience stores or station machines, which makes it quick and easy to keep moving. I also bought an AREX ticket to get from the airport into Seoul centre. There are two options, express and all-stop, with roughly ten minutes difference in journey time and around KRW5,000 difference in price. I went with the all-stop.

For the KTX, I booked through the KORAIL app. One thing worth knowing, if you tap the print button within the app it marks the ticket as printed and you can't reopen it afterwards, so make sure you save a PDF copy somewhere on your phone as a backup. You'll only need to show it if someone else is sitting in your seat and the conductor asks, which only came up a handful of times across the whole trip. I travelled Seoul to Gyeongju, Gyeongju to Busan, Busan to Jeonju and Jeonju back to Seoul, with the full set of KTX tickets coming to £80.

Outside of Seoul, make more use of taxis than you might expect to need. A 10- to 20-minute journey typically cost around KRW9,000, which is next to nothing, and most are linked with Uber, making the whole thing far easier than it sounds. For the KTX stations themselves, give yourself time to spare, but they're nowhere near the scale of some of the bigger Shinkansen stations in Japan, so there's less risk of getting lost and less need to arrive as early as you would there.

Itinerary

Seoul · 5 days

I stayed at New Blanc Central Myeongdong for five nights at £405. Not slap in the middle of Myeongdong itself, more towards the east in the grittier stretch near Euljiro, but the hotel itself was excellent, modern, well-equipped rooms, a shared cooking space and a rooftop bar as a nice added touch.

I headed straight into Seoul on arrival and grabbed some food before check-in at 2pm. Once in, I showered, unpacked a few bits and went out to Cheonggyecheon, the stream that runs through the centre of the city. It had a nice atmosphere for an evening wander. I took some photos, grabbed food and headed back to crash.

Day two started with breakfast at Tous Les Jours, well worth a visit for the selection on offer, before heading to Gyeongbokgung Palace to catch the changing of the guard ceremony and have a wander round. Plenty of people in hanbok rentals posing for photos across the grounds. From there I walked over to Bukchon Hanok Village, where the rain started, heavy enough that I decided to find a shopping centre to wait it out rather than push on. Worth knowing the Hanok village has enforced visiting hours, so it's worth checking these before planning around it.

Day three I took the subway over to Gangnam, visiting COEX Mall and Bongeunsa Temple across the road. The Starfield Library inside COEX was a genuinely cool thing to see, even with the crowds. Bongeunsa had its lanterns out for Yeondeunghoe, the Buddhist lantern festival, which made for a colourful and peaceful wander before heading back to Myeongdong. Once back, I decided to hike up Namsan Park to the N Seoul Tower just before sunset. I picked up a ticket through Klook at £10, noticeably cheaper than buying direct even with their discount offer. The views were good, especially once the lights came on across the city, though reflections made it genuinely difficult to get a clean photo, worth bearing in mind if you're going up for photography specifically.

Day four was a 5:30am start for a DMZ day tour. The ticket, booked through Klook at £38, included pickup from Myeongdong subway entrance at 7am and had me back by around 2 to 2:30pm, very good value for what it covered. The tour itself was genuinely interesting, walking through the history of the Korean War and feeling the tension that still exists at the Dora Observatory. Photography is mostly prohibited unless you're told otherwise, so there isn't much I can share from the experience itself. The tunnel was a strange one too, and considerably harder coming back up than it was going down.

My final day I spent the early afternoon wandering Hongdae, then watched it come alive in the evening with buskers and street performers. A really good atmosphere, everyone gathered round watching, cheering or singing along.

Seoul Honest Take

Seoul didn't quite live up to the social media image you see online. It's grittier and rougher round the edges than expected, and nowhere near as clean as it's often portrayed, though whether that's down to tourist areas specifically or the city as a whole is hard to say. The food markets felt like something of a tourist trap too. There's a restaurant that's held a Michelin Bib Gourmand for over ten years running, serving Korean dumplings for KRW16,000, while the street food stalls nearby ask around KRW12,000 for similar. I know where I'd rather spend my money.

That said, the city has genuine highlights. Gyeongbokgung, the Namsan hike at sunset, and the DMZ tour in particular all earned their place on the itinerary, and Hongdae's evening atmosphere was one of the better surprises of the trip.

Itinerary

Gyeongju · 2 days

I stayed at Gyeongju GG Tourist Hotel for two nights at £167.38. Great location wise, close enough to walk, taxi or catch the local bus to most attractions, though the hotel itself was a little dated and the bed was genuinely uncomfortable.

On arrival I checked in, grabbed some food, then headed out for a walk to Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond as the evening drew in. Entrance came to around £2 to £3. I made my way back via Woljeonggyo Bridge, which looked incredible lit up at night, the painted detail and engraved pattern across it standing out properly once darkness set in.

The following day I visited the tombs, busy but not excessively so, which meant I could take my time wandering through the grounds without fighting for space. From there I had a look around the Hanok Village area in the centre and the shops, getting photos of the traditional but modernised buildings along the way. The heat was fairly intense that day, so I headed back for a mid-afternoon break before heading back out for golden hour. Crowds were thinning by then, food was good, and the evening had a nice atmosphere, a striking contrast to the buzz and busyness of Seoul.

My final morning was a travel day through to Busan. I got ready, stored my luggage, went for one last wander round, then picked my bags back up and caught the bus to the station.

Gyeongju Honest Take

Gyeongju was a cool stop, lots of photo opportunities without having to try too hard, and steeped in history throughout. I liked the traditional buildings with their modern renovation twist, and the tombs are scattered so widely across the grounds that they almost become the norm once you clock what you're looking at. It's a clean, chilled city overall. Two days felt like the sweet spot here. Most tours booked locally push you towards the coast or Busan as day trips, and with Busan already on the itinerary separately, there wasn't much left to add by staying longer.

The local bus was my first real experience of Korea's local bus network specifically, and while it was efficient and cheap, it felt like enough of a rat race that I came away wondering if brake pad replacement is a weekly job for the drivers.

Itinerary

Busan · 4 days

I stayed at Uniqstay for four nights at £170. The hotel felt properly tucked away, and once you found it, it stood out like a sore thumb, low-rise rather than high-rise, and sitting in more of a working area than a tourist strip. That said, it was only minutes on foot from the stations and the heart of Busan itself. The staff were really friendly and made a good effort to make you feel welcome. The room was a decent size, maybe slightly in need of a refresh if being picky, but I'd happily stay here again based on the experience.

After checking in I headed out for a wander and some food, then made my way over to Haeundae Beach. I'd hoped to catch the sunset but misjudged the journey length and arrived just after it had gone. The beach was busy, and I happened to catch the Haeundae Sand Festival while I was there, some of the sculptures on display were genuinely incredible. There was also a concert playing on the beach, and the whole place had a real buzz to it. The contrast of multi storey hotels lining the shore behind it all felt like something out of a film, well worth a visit.

Day two I headed over to the Skyluge. This was something I'd wanted to do in New Zealand, but the weather wasn't playing ball. Given it's the same company, this felt like a second chance. I picked up a 2 Experiences Busan pass via Klook, costing £23, and opted for the luge, though I hadn't realised the Marine Cable Car needed booking the day before to guarantee entry, so I only ended up using the one activity. The luge itself was great fun, the pass gets you four rides down across four different routes, so you get to experience them all. It's also notably cheaper booked through Klook, worth bearing in mind if you're planning the same.

In the evening I hiked up to Hwangnyeongsan Observatory. You can get a taxi up for a small fee, and in hindsight I wish I had, it's a steep climb. The views make it worthwhile, though it does get busy, so patience is needed once you're up there. I got a taxi back down, costing around KRW12,000, which felt like money well spent after the climb up.

Day three I headed over to Gamcheon Culture Village and had a wander round and through the village itself. You can take the bus using your T-money card, or walk up if you fancy the hills. It's a bit of a task, but worth it once you reach the top given the viewpoints. Worth flagging, the stairs in and around the village are a bit all over the place and uneven, so bear this in mind if you're unsteady on your feet or uneasy with heights. I'd planned to use my cable car ticket this day but realised it needed booking the day before to validate, and with the following day's weather looking poor and nothing else worth doing lined up, I let it lapse.

In the evening I headed over to Samgwangsa Temple, timed with their Lotus Lantern Festival for Buddha's Birthday, so the whole site was lit up in colour. I grabbed a taxi up given the hills and hikes across both this day and the previous one, costing around KRW10,000. There was plenty to see, and with it being dark, the lanterns and temples lit up across the grounds made for a genuinely striking evening. Definitely worth building into a Busan stop.

Day four brought really bad weather that hung around all day, so I didn't get up to much. I'd planned to spend the day at Gwangalli Beach, but the weather put a stop to that. Instead I headed over to the Lotte Mall near the hotel and had a wander through the floors, a chilled, low-key day by comparison to the rest of the trip.

Busan Honest Take

Busan wasn't quite what I expected. It was something of a logistical nightmare in all honesty, with most attractions spread right across the city and a minimum of an hour to get between locations. Given how busy it gets, it's worth booking attractions well in advance too, most things were filling up at least a couple of weeks out. I'd wanted to do the Busan Sky Capsule, for example, but it was already sold out by the time I looked. With the city spread out the way it is, everything felt like harder work than it should have been, and without proper planning your day can disappear entirely to travel time.

Itinerary

Jeonju · 1 day

I arrived into Jeonju around 5:30pm, having taken a fairly circuitous route given the KTX doesn't serve the station directly. The journey took around five hours with a change in between. By the time I landed, the rain had properly set in, making for a miserable arrival all round. I grabbed a taxi to the hotel, costing around KRW11,000.

I stayed at Shilla Stay Jeonju, costing £55, excellent value for the price, an electric bed, modern rooms and great amenities throughout. It was well located too, just a short walk from the Hanok Village. Once the weather dried up I grabbed some food and called it a night.

Before heading back to Incheon the next morning, I visited the Hanok Village and stopped at Gyeonggijeon Shrine, entry costing KRW1,000. Well worth it for the price, offering a proper insight into Korea's history under the Joseon kings. I grabbed a taxi back to the station, another KRW11,000, and headed back towards Seoul.

Jeonju Honest Take

Jeonju was rich with history and made for an interesting stop. That said, given the logistics of getting there, I probably wouldn't recommend it on a typical loop of the country. A full day is enough, though it's also weather dependent, so worth factoring that in when deciding whether it earns a place on your itinerary. It carries a similar vibe to Gyeongju too, so if that's already on your route, Jeonju is arguably the smaller version and one you could reasonably skip.

Itinerary

Seoul/Incheon · 1 day

I stayed at Ibis Incheon Airport for the final night, costing around £85. The room was great for the price, spacious and well equipped, with the added convenience of being close to the airport ahead of the flight. I spent the afternoon back in Seoul, grabbed some food, then caught the AREX ticket back to Incheon around 7pm, ready for a midday flight home the following day.

Seoul/Incheon Honest Take

I deliberately booked this final night close to the airport given the headache I'd had in Japan when BA cancelled my flight a few hours before it was scheduled. Thankfully there weren't any issues this time round, but I'd still recommend staying near the airport on your last night regardless, in case your flight schedule changes or you're rebooked onto something different. It gives you more flexibility to adjust plans without the added stress of being further out. Largely a contingency built into the itinerary based on past experience rather than anything specific to this trip.

Wrap Up

A Final Word On South Korea

South Korea left me feeling somewhat underwhelmed if I'm honest. I'm glad I went, and there were experiences along the way I'd genuinely recommend, but having already been to Japan I think it's spoilt me for this trip, even though the two countries are entirely different. The DMZ stood out as a genuine highlight, fascinating from a historical perspective and leaving a strange atmosphere as you move through the checkpoints and have your passport checked.

If you haven't been to either country, my honest recommendation based on this trip would be to visit South Korea first and Japan second, rather than the other way round. I also think South Korea is heavily hyped on social media, and a lot of the content shared out there oversells the destination compared to what you actually experience on the ground.

I'm glad I visited, but I don't think I'd go back.
